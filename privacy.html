Privacy

Last updated 21 August 2026

Waffle is a photo app for a small group of friends. It works because the photos stay between you. This page says exactly what is collected, who can see it, and how to get rid of it, in plain language, because a policy nobody can read protects nobody.

The short version

  • No analytics, no trackers, no ads. There is no third-party SDK of any kind in the app. Nobody is measuring what you tap.
  • Your photos are private to your friends. Not by a setting you could get wrong: the rule is enforced in the database on every single request, including the image files.
  • Nothing is sold or shared for advertising. Ever. There is no business model that would want it.
  • Delete your account and the photos really go: the image files, not just the rows pointing at them.
  • No location data. The app never asks for it, and photo location metadata is deliberately switched off.

Who runs this

Waffle is made by Batter, a one-person project based in San Jose, California. Questions, requests, or complaints: andrewbaobui@gmail.com. A real person reads that address.

What Waffle collects

When you make an account

Either an email address and password, or a sign-in from Apple or Google. If you use Apple, Apple may give Waffle a relay address rather than your real one. That is Apple's feature, and it works fine here. Google supplies your email, name, and profile picture.

You also choose a handle and a display name, and may add a bio and a profile picture.

Your time zone

The app reads your device's time zone and stores it. This is not tracking. It is how the app knows when your day ends, so a photo taken at 11pm counts for that day and not the next. It updates when your phone's time zone changes.

What you make in the app

  • Photos. Taken live from the camera. Two sizes are stored: a full square and a small thumbnail.
  • Waffles: their names, colours, and any reminder time you set.
  • Comments, hearts, and comment likes.
  • Who you are friends with, who you have invited to a shared waffle, and anyone you have muted or blocked.
  • Reports, if you report a post. These include who reported what, so they can be acted on.

What Waffle does not collect

No location. No contacts. No advertising identifier. No analytics, crash reporting, or attribution SDK. The app has none installed. No browsing history, and no data from any other app on your phone.

On photo metadata. Photos are re-encoded by the app before they are uploaded, and location tagging is explicitly disabled. The picture that leaves your phone does not carry where it was taken.

Where it is stored

Accounts, content, and photos are hosted on Supabase, a managed database and storage provider. Photos live in a private storage bucket that is not reachable without a signed link generated for someone the rules already allow.

This website is hosted on Vercel, and loads its fonts from Google Fonts, which means Google sees the IP address of anyone loading this page, as it would on most of the web. The site sets no cookies and runs no analytics.

If you join the waitlist, your email address is stored so you can be told when the app launches. That list cannot be read by the key this page uses; it can only be added to.

What stays on your phone

Some things never leave your device: your light/dark preference, whether you have seen the intro, your notification settings, and any photo that has not finished uploading yet. Notifications are generated on your phone. Waffle has no push server, so no notification you receive has passed through anyone's hands.

If you allow it, Waffle also saves a copy of each photo you take into your own camera roll. That copy is yours and is unaffected by anything you do in the app.

Permissions the app asks for

Deleting things

One photo

A photo can be deleted for 24 hours after you post it. After that it is permanent. That is the whole idea of the app, and it is enforced in the database rather than by hiding a button. Deleting your account is the way to remove a photo that has set.

Your whole account

Settings → Delete account. This removes your profile, every waffle you started, every photo you posted, your comments and hearts, and your friendships. The image files are deleted too, not only the records pointing at them.

Two honest caveats. Photos you added to a shared waffle are deleted with your account, but the waffle itself survives for the people still in it. And a comment you left on someone else's photo goes with your account, which may leave a gap in a conversation they can still see.

Deletion is immediate and cannot be undone. If you would rather have a copy first, save your photos from inside the app before you delete.

Children

Waffle is not directed at children under 13 and accounts are not knowingly created for them. If you believe a child has made an account, email andrewbaobui@gmail.com and it will be removed.

Your rights

Wherever you live, you can ask what is held about you, ask for a copy, ask for it to be corrected, or ask for it to be deleted. The Delete account button does the last one immediately; for anything else, email andrewbaobui@gmail.com.

If you are in California, the CCPA gives you those rights explicitly, plus the right not to be discriminated against for using them. Waffle has never sold or shared personal information, so there is nothing to opt out of. If you are in the UK or EU, the lawful basis for handling your content is the contract between you and Waffle: you asked it to store your photos so your friends could see them.

Security, honestly

Everything travels over encrypted connections and is encrypted at rest by the hosting provider. Access rules are enforced by the database rather than by the app, which means a modified or broken copy of the app still cannot reach someone else's photos.

That said: this is a small project run by one person, and no system is perfect. If you find a security problem, email andrewbaobui@gmail.com and it will be taken seriously.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, the date at the top changes and anyone on the waitlist or using the app will be told. Old versions of this page are kept in the project's public history.
